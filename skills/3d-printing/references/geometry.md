# Geometry lessons

These are decision aids, not universal tolerances. Read the project's record for exact dimensions, current revision, and physical feedback.

## Grips and level holders

- Grip flips and cup opening direction are separate transforms. Earlier “upward” placement meant XY motion without a Z rise; establish the view each time.
- Protect launcher interfaces and finger or palm contact surfaces. Keeping source material does not prevent additions from obstructing finger wrap; compare silhouettes and added-material intrusion separately.
- Preserve a successful bore when changing cup depth or the connector. The measured 31.66 mm level previously fit a 31.8 mm opening; this applies to that project only.
- Recess depth = total holder height minus floor thickness. For a flush rim, total height = level height plus floor thickness. Push-out openings need remaining floor support.
- Strengthen connectors outside contact keepouts. Check wall thickness at chamfers as well as midsections; do not infer strength from watertightness or nominal perimeter counts.

## Trays and organizers

- Measure clear inside widths, not divider spacing. Confirm actual file identity and geometry before interpreting 4U/5U names.
- A tray's height axis may be tilted. Preserve the floor and grid interface when raising walls; arrange the intended flat underside on the bed.
- Reallocating slot widths or stepped holder heights must preserve nearby tubes, ledges, diameters, and unaffected regions. Check slices and surface displacement outside the edit.

## Clips

- Separate leg rotation from the angle between opening directions: a 45° upward leg can leave openings 135° apart. Use a labeled cross-section.
- Flat channels require planar parallel walls and a consistent profile along the part. Bow, detents, and joint funnels can change effective clearance.
- A chosen undersize press fit is a project decision; verify it with a short test section rather than assuming an FDM shrink allowance.

## Export and physical checks

Reload the actual STL and check connected components, watertightness, winding/volume where supported, dimensions, functional openings, and protected regions. Tiny collapsed export faces can invalidate an otherwise sound in-memory mesh. Bound any repair or simplification, record its tolerance and shape/volume impact, and repeat checks. Keep numerical repair distinct from intentional design changes.

Use small bore, clip, or tray-section prints when useful. Record physical clearance, feel, strength, and fit separately from geometry validation; preserve uncertainty where the tested variant is unknown.
